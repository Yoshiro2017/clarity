Clarity

A professional, thoughtfully designed digital space built on principles of clarity, accessibility, and respectful design. Every element — from the comprehensive selection of typefaces to the palette, spacing, and interaction patterns — has been chosen with deliberate intent, balancing aesthetic refinement with functional utility.

This is not a commercial platform. There are no advertisements, no tracking scripts, no analytics, and no third-party monitoring. This space exists to present information, share ideas, and demonstrate thoughtful web design in a manner that respects your attention, your time, and your privacy.

The foundation is built entirely on open standards and open-source technology. The Noto font family provides comprehensive typographic support — from global Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, and Arabic scripts to historical and regional writing systems — with consistent quality across devices and languages. The codebase is minimal, self-contained, and designed to be understood, modified, and extended by anyone without requiring specialized tools or proprietary systems.

Learn About These Scripts

What's Luwian, Ahom, Adlam or Arabic?

Each writing system included here represents a distinct cultural and historical tradition. Below is an overview of their origins, characteristics, and current status.

Arabic

Arabic is one of the world's most widely used writing systems, serving as the primary script for more than 420 million people across the Middle East, North Africa, and beyond. Originating in the Arabian Peninsula in pre-Islamic times, it evolved into a major literary and religious vehicle from the 7th century onward. Written from right to left, it is a cursive script where letters connect, with forms changing depending on position within a word. It has developed distinct calligraphic traditions — including Kufi, Naskh, Ruq'ah, and Thuluth — each suited to different purposes. Beyond the Arabic language itself, the script has been adapted to write Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Kurdish, and many other languages.

Three distinct Noto families demonstrate its range: Sans Arabic for modern clarity and daily use, Kufi Arabic for geometric display, and Naskh Arabic for traditional, flowing legibility.

Source: Wikipedia — Arabic

Luwian / Anatolian Hieroglyphs

Also known as Luwian hieroglyphs, this was the primary writing system of the Luwian people and the Hittite Empire across ancient Anatolia and northern Syria, used from roughly 1400 to 700 BCE. Unlike alphabetic systems, it is a mixed script combining logographic symbols and syllabic signs. Over 500 distinct signs have been identified. Deciphered during the 20th century, these inscriptions record royal deeds, religious dedications, and administrative records. The script fell out of use as alphabetic writing systems spread through the region, leaving a valuable archaeological record of languages and cultures otherwise lost to time.

Noto Sans Anatolian Hieroglyphs renders these ancient symbols with care, preserving their monumental visual character for modern display and education.

Sources: Wikipedia — Luwian language; Anatolian hieroglyphs

Ahom

The Ahom script was the traditional writing system of the Ahom people, who established and ruled the Ahom Kingdom in what is now Upper Assam, northeastern India, for nearly six centuries — from the 13th until the 19th century. Used to record royal chronicles, religious texts, and administrative edicts, it is closely related to other Southeast Asian scripts of Tibeto-Burman origin. The Ahom language and script declined under British colonial rule and were long considered extinct. In recent decades, however, there has been significant cultural and educational revival, with communities actively teaching and revitalizing both the language and its writing system.

Noto Serif Ahom supports this revitalization by providing a clean, digital typeface that honors the script's handwritten, flowing heritage.

Source: Wikipedia — Ahom language

Adlam

Adlam is a modern writing system invented in the late 1980s by brothers Ibrahima and Abdoulaye Barry for the Fulani (Fulɓe) peoples of West and Central Africa. Created to be phonetically precise, visually distinct, and culturally empowering, it is written from right to left and has its own unique alphabet with no direct derivation from Arabic or Latin scripts. Remarkably, it was developed in modern times and has grown steadily in adoption — used in books, signage, education, and community projects across Guinea, Nigeria, Senegal, Mali, and beyond. Two forms exist: a joined cursive style for daily writing and publications, and an unjoined separated style designed specifically for learners and beginners.

Noto Sans Adlam and Noto Sans Adlam Unjoined support both forms, reflecting the script's dual identity as a living cursive and an educational tool.

Source: Wikipedia — Adlam script

Inclusion as Design

These scripts are not curiosities. They represent living heritage, revived identity, and continuous cultural expression. Including them alongside Latin, Cyrillic, and Greek is a deliberate choice: digital spaces belong to all the world's writing traditions, not only the most widely encountered. When typefaces are available and properly implemented, every script can be clear, legible, and dignified on screen.

Respect in design means seeing — and serving — everyone.

About This Project

This project began as an exploration of what a clean, sustainable, and respectful website could be. In an era where many digital spaces are cluttered, opaque, or built primarily around monetization, there is value in creating something that prioritizes the visitor above all else. Clarity is the result of that exploration: a template, a demonstration, and a living document of thoughtful web practice.

Origins and Motivation

The impetus came from observing how the modern web has evolved. Heavy frameworks, excessive dependencies, opaque tracking mechanisms, and intrusive advertising have become commonplace, often at the expense of speed, readability, and user trust. These burdens fall disproportionately on visitors with limited bandwidth, older devices, assistive technologies, or constrained data plans. A return to fundamentals — semantic HTML, modest CSS, and unobtrusive scripting — is not a regression; it is a deliberate choice toward efficiency, longevity, and inclusivity.

Every decision made here flows from a single premise: the purpose of a website is to communicate. All other elements — styling, navigation, animation — exist to serve that purpose, never to obscure it. Content leads; presentation supports; functionality enhances.

Typography as Inclusion

The inclusion of diverse writing systems is not merely decorative. It is a statement that digital spaces belong to everyone, regardless of what writing system their language uses. Arabic — led by Noto Sans Arabic as the primary choice, with Kufi and Naskh in supporting roles — represents one of the world's most widely used and historically significant calligraphic and typographic traditions. Anatolian Hieroglyphs connect visitors to the ancient cultural heritage of Anatolia. Ahom preserves a script with living legacy in Northeast India. Adlam — in both joined and unjoined forms — supports a modern revitalized writing system for the Fulani peoples across West and Central Africa.

When these scripts are treated with the same care and typographic quality as Latin, Cyrillic, or Greek, we acknowledge the full diversity of human expression. Good design is universal by choice, not by assumption.

Guiding Principles

Three core principles guide every aspect of this work:

Legibility First. Text is the primary medium. Typefaces are selected for readability at their intended sizes; contrast is calibrated to meet established accessibility standards; spacing is tuned to guide the eye without distraction.

Transparency. The code is as readable as the content. Structure is semantic; styling is organized; behavior is predictable. Nothing is hidden, and no code runs without clear purpose.

Respect. Your attention, device, and privacy are valued. No resources are loaded unnecessarily; no data is collected; no time is wasted on elements that do not serve you.

Typography

The Noto family provides a unified, high-quality set of faces designed for global readability — with distinct variants tuned for specific roles and writing systems.

Arabic — Primary and Alternatives

Noto Sans Arabic is the primary Arabic typeface throughout this project. Clean, modern, and highly legible at paragraph sizes, it balances traditional cursive connections with simplified forms suited to digital interfaces and screens. It is the recommended choice for general Arabic text.

Noto Kufi Arabic — Geometric and monumental. Used for display, titles, and decorative purposes where presence and structure matter more than extended reading.

Noto Naskh Arabic — Traditional and fluid. The classic book form, optimized for comfort over long passages. Used where a formal or literary tone is appropriate.

All three share identical sample text in the homepage display to make direct visual comparison straightforward.

Primary Families

Noto Sans Display / Noto Serif Display — headings and titles, spacing optimized for larger sizes.

Noto Sans — default body text, highly legible.

Noto Serif — long-form, articles, quoted material.

Noto Sans Mono — code, notation, fixed-width content.

Specialized Script Families

Anatolian Hieroglyphs — ancient Luwian script.

Ahom — historical Assamese script.

Adlam Joined / Unjoined — modern Fulani script, both forms supported.

Design Philosophy

The visual design follows a restrained, professional aesthetic built on spaciousness, clear hierarchy, and consistent rhythm. There are no decorative flourishes without function; every element earns its place through utility or clarity.

Color System

The palette is minimal and purpose-built. A neutral foundation ensures maximum readability, while a single accent color provides consistent focal points across interactive elements and headings. Contrast ratios meet WCAG standards, and the theme adapts automatically to system preferences or manual selection.

Light mode uses a clean white background with dark gray text for optimal reading conditions. Dark mode employs a deep neutral background with soft white text, reducing eye strain in low-light environments without sacrificing legibility. Transitions between modes are smooth and unobtrusive.

Layout and Rhythm

Content is contained within a reasonable maximum width to prevent eye fatigue on large screens. Margins and padding are consistent across sections, creating a visual rhythm that guides navigation. Headings are separated from body text by generous spacing, reinforcing the document structure and making scanning intuitive.

The layout adapts fluidly to screen size. Navigation reorganizes for smaller devices; line lengths adjust to remain comfortable; typography scales appropriately. No content is hidden, and no functionality is removed at any breakpoint.

Script Considerations

Right-to-left writing systems such as Arabic and Adlam are rendered with natural directionality. Noto Sans Arabic serves as the standard Arabic face across the site, with Kufi and Naskh available when a specific stylistic tone is needed. Connected scripts benefit from natural cursive flow without forced separation. Specialized families preserve authentic character shapes, spacing, and aesthetic intent.
